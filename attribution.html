Case study Summer 2026 Mobile truck tire service

Making a phone call measurable

A roadside truck tire business had run for almost a year with barely a job a day. Three ad platforms each reported a fraction of what was happening. None could answer the only question that mattered: did the call turn into work?

§ 01 Situation

Almost no calls, in the season that should have been busiest

Mobile tire service for semi and commercial trucks — roadside work, where a stranded driver searches once and calls whoever answers. Demand isn't the constraint. Being findable at the moment of the search is.

The owner rarely did more than a job a day. It was summer, which ruled out seasonality. Ads pointed at a Google Business Profile; there was no website, so there was nowhere to place conversion tracking and no landing experience to tune.

Every measurement decision that followed depended on first having somewhere to measure.

§ 02 Sequence

The order mattered

Each step was a precondition for the next.

01 Built the customer-facing website A real landing destination for paid traffic, and the first surface capable of hosting conversion tracking.
02 Rebuilt Google Ads, added Local Services Ads Phrase-match search on semi and commercial tire queries, Performance Max, and LSA.
03 Found no unified tracking Three platforms reporting separately, in incompatible formats, with no way to see a call end to end.
04 Built the attribution layer One row per call, joining platform data to the outcome the owner recorded by hand.
05 Used it to move the budget Cut spend on traffic that didn't convert; pushed budget toward what did.
§ 03 Problem

Three systems, three partial truths

GA4 Site sessions and tel: click events — a real conversion on a business that runs on calls. Tracing a tap back to its source was difficult and often impossible.
Google Ads Its own call reporting, tied to campaigns and keywords. Blind to anything it didn't serve.
Local Services Ads Lead records in a separate console with its own ratings. Doesn't join to GA4 or Ads at all.

Left alone this produces two failures at once: the same call counted twice by two systems, and calls missing from all of them. Neither error is visible from inside any one platform — the dashboards look fine.

And the deeper gap: all three measure calls. The business makes money on jobs. A cost-conscious owner asked to spend more isn't asking how many phones rang.

§ 04 Build

One row per call

Built in Google Sheets, fed by a Google Form — a deliberate choice over anything more sophisticated. The outcome data could only come from the person answering the phone, so adoption was the binding constraint. A tool the owner fills in between jobs beats a better one he abandons.

Design · Joining three sources to an outcome Owner-entered data
Join key

Phone number normalized to ten raw digits. Each platform formats numbers differently, so matching on the raw string silently drops rows and produces a report that looks complete. Stripping to digits turned three incompatible exports into one joinable key.

Aggregation

COUNTIFS and SUMIFS into Outcomes and Channels tabs, so calls could be read by date, by source, and by what they became. Charts built on that data made gaps and trends visible directly.

Self-extension

ARRAYFORMULA and open chart ranges, so new form submissions extend the report automatically. Anything needing a formula dragged down would have gone stale within a week.

Outcome

The owner recorded what the call was and whether it became work — the same details he still records on job forms today. That column is the one thing no platform could supply.

Known limitation

Outcome data is self-reported and hand-entered, so it carries the usual risks. It was reconciled against platform call counts to catch gaps, but it isn't instrumented ground truth. It was accurate enough to make budget decisions with, which was the bar it needed to clear.

§ 05 Result

The measurement is what unlocked the spend

The owner was reluctant to commit $300 a month. Within one to two months — call volume visibly climbing, each call traceable to a source and an outcome — he was spending more than ten times that across channels, confidently.

An owner who won't spend $300 because he can't see what it returns isn't being stubborn. He's responding correctly to missing information.

That decision is the strongest evidence the system worked, and better evidence than a figure would be. Nobody multiplies their ad spend tenfold on a channel they can't see returning.

On what isn't claimed here

No revenue or profit figures appear on this page. The business had no measured baseline before this engagement, so any before-and-after comparison would be invented. The prior state is the owner's characterization; the spend increase is a decision I observed.

§ 06 Ongoing

The problem it surfaced next

Better measurement produces better problems. With calls classified by outcome, the leak became visible: budget meant for semi tire work was going to passenger vehicle traffic, and to searchers who landed on the page and left because it didn't show what they were looking for. Mostly that meant no call at all — spend with nothing on the other end.

Active work: tightening phrase-match targeting and extending negative keyword lists against a 33-keyword positive set, plus landing page changes so the page answers what the search actually asked. Then watching the outcome column to confirm the mix shifts rather than assuming it did.

§ 07 Consequence

Why this became software

Running the Sheets system revealed how much was tracked by hand elsewhere:

  • Call attribution in a Google Form feeding a spreadsheet
  • Invoices in separate third-party software
  • Tire inventory counted manually
  • Quotes written by hand, unlinked to the job they became
  • No expense tracking and no customer accounts at all

Not five problems — five views of one event. A call becomes a job, the job consumes tires, produces an invoice, generates expenses, and belongs to a customer. Modeling that event once collapses all five.
